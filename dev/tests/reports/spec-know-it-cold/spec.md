## Aim (the feeling to elicit)
Students feel why knowing 1600–1900 cold matters: in the HSC, marks go to precise, quotable evidence (time stamp or bar + element of music + what it does + why it belongs to its era). Vague general answers lose marks. "Playing the HSC game."

## Language rules (Justin's)
- "Elements of music" (NOT "concepts"): duration, pitch, dynamics and expressive techniques, tone colour, texture, structure.
- Australian spelling. Plain, direct, warm, not AI-sounding. No em-dash overuse.
- Strict common-practice theory kept distinct; functional harmony terms used accurately.

## Quote formula
Time stamp (in the named recording) or bar number → element of music → what happens → why it is typical of the era. Example: "0:12, pitch (harmony): parallel ninth chords with no V–I, so the harmony is colour, not function: Impressionist, not Romantic."

## Weekly loop (about 2–3 hours a week)
Listen (5 landmarks, fixed recordings) · Quote Bank (3 quotable moments per work, with time stamps from the named recording AND bar numbers from the score where possible) · Sing it (one moment a week from memory, phone recording) · Write (one paragraph a week; Week 6 a full extended response) · Optional: walk the week's Garden journey.

## Weeks
1 Early Baroque: from mode to key (works 1–5) · 2 Late Baroque: function, counterpoint, ritornello (6–12) · 3 Classical: balance, sonata form, the orchestra (13–18) · 4 Beethoven into early Romantic: the individual voice (19–24) · 5 Late Romantic and the 1900 line: the trap round (25–30 + boundary) · 6 Synthesis: Pin the Era self-test + "Era Defence" extended response.

## The 1900 line (content rule)
For Music 2's 1600–1900 topic, a work must be inside the dates AND show the period's harmonic language. Always say which date you use: composition, first performance, revision or publication.
